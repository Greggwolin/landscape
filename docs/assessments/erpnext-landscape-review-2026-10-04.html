What would have to be done

  1. Choose and operate a maintained ERPNext edition. Your current copy is an unfinished development edition from June. The comparison found no separate custom changes. It supplies starting software, but the real estate tailoring still needs to be done. Verified comparison.
  2. Agree how records correspond. Connect legal entities, projects, areas, phases, cost categories, and vendors. A development project may involve several legal entities, so these connections need explicit rules.
  3. Make transfers trustworthy. Keep links to the original records, detect duplicates, handle corrected or cancelled bills, and check that totals agree. A bill and its later payment must not count twice as the same cost.
  4. Choose the visible experience. Importing selected information is a smaller commitment than putting bill entry, purchasing, approvals, and accounting reports inside Landscape.
  5. Tailor the real estate workflows that matter. Requirements might include retainage, change orders, lender draws, lien waivers, and allocating costs across entities or phases. Each needs a specific review against what ERPNext already supports.
  6. Plan ongoing ownership. Someone must own accounting setup, customer access, backups, software updates, failed transfers, and support. Review the license if customized software will be distributed to customers.

Timesheets could matter for internal labor allocation or billed management services. Asset accounting could matter for owned equipment or retained fixed assets. These depend on the business model rather than being universally unnecessary.

The strongest overlap is accounting records, vendor bills and payments, purchasing, project cost classification, budgets, and financial reporting. ERPNext’s broad feature list still needs a separate review for development-specific contract and payment requirements.
