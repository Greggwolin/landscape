Show the Working

Calculation audit · design for Gregg · 2026-10-14 · replaces this morning's "Deal Ledger" paper, which designed a record of who changed what; this one designs how each number was computed, which is the audit you asked for. The technical version is the companion file for the coding assistant.

What it is. Every figure the app computes can open its working: the numbers that went into it, the rule that combined them, and the result, down to the stored values a person typed or a document supplied. Double-click a cash-flow cell and see the lines that sum to it; open one of those and see the budget item, the spread pattern and the inflation that produced it; open the IRR tile and see the dated series the rate was solved on. Landscaper answers "how did you get 39.1%?" from the same working, so the words and the screen can never disagree.

Why it is the novelty. A spreadsheet shows its formulas but not its reasoning, and ARGUS shows neither. A general assistant can produce a number but cannot prove it. Landscape computing the figure and showing the chain beneath it, in plain words, is the thing a Kern board member can check without trusting anyone. It is also what makes the AI's number defensible, which is the strategy Mike said they need.

What is already there

Checked in the code this afternoon, on the path the Cash Flow screen actually runs.

  • One engine builds the land cash flow. It already knows, for every line, which budget item or which parcel it came from, and it already computes the spread weights, the inflation factor and the price escalation for each month. It computes them and throws them away; only the final amount per month is kept.
  • The returns are computed from that same run: the IRR on the actual dates of the monthly flows (your Oct 1 decision), the NPV at the discount rate, the equity multiple as cash in over cash out, peak equity as the deepest point of the running total. Again, the series and the method are known at the moment of computing and not kept.
  • The budget schedule already has a working panel: double-click a line and it shows quantity × rate = total. That is the seed. It covers one layer of one schedule.
  • Where a number was typed or extracted is not recorded (this morning's finding). That stays true and is a later layer, not this one: the working stops at "stored value: lot price $185,000, parcel 1.101" and does not yet say who put it there.

Every underlined-looking "stored:" line is a real value in the database. Later, when the change record exists, that line also says who entered it and when. Nothing in this design has to be redone for that.

How it gets built

Five slices. The engine learns to keep its working once; every screen and Landscaper then read it. Each slice is run by you locally before it goes live.

  1. The engine keeps its working. Instead of discarding the spread weights, inflation factors, escalation and deductions, each line and each month records what produced it. The cash-flow run gets slower by very little, because the numbers are already computed; they are just no longer thrown away. Nothing on screen changes yet.
  2. The cash-flow cell panel. Double-click a cell, see the working. Costs and parcel revenue first; subtotals and the financing lines next.
  3. The returns tiles. IRR, NPV, multiple, peak equity each open their method and series, with the IRR cross-checked by a second method on the same series.
  4. Landscaper reads the working. One new ability: given a cell or a tile, return its working in words. The answer to "how did you get this" stops being Landscaper's own reasoning.
  5. The budget's second layer and the report appendix.

Slices 1 to 3 are the demo. After them, every number on the two screens you would show Kern can be opened to its stored inputs.

The case against. Slice 1 edits the one engine everything depends on, the canonical cascading-change risk in this app. Mitigation: the working is added beside the existing numbers, never in place of them, and the first success test is that every existing figure is unchanged to the cent. What would make me wrong: if prospects trust the number once they see the inputs screen and never open the working, this is a feature for the one skeptic in the room. I think the skeptic is the one who signs.

What it is not. Not a record of who typed a value or when (this morning's paper; it becomes the layer beneath this one). Not a formula editor. Not a way to change a number from inside the panel.
