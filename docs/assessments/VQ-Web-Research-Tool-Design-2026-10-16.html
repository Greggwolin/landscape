What I propose to build

Two plain abilities, inside the app, usable before a project exists:

  1. Search the web — a query, optionally limited to one site (e.g. the county's), returning titles, links and snippets.
  2. Read a page — fetch a web page or PDF and return its text, so Landscaper quotes the actual staff report rather than a headline.

Plus three rules that make it safe to trust:

  • Every web fact carries its source — site and date in the sentence, the link once. A search snippet alone only proves "a page with this title exists"; it never becomes a fact until the page is read.
  • Nothing retrieved means "nothing retrieved." A blocked or empty page is reported as such. (This is the hole the invented "Zonda appraisal" fell through today.)
  • It fires when you ask for research — "look up", "research", "what does the county say", "is this still approved" — or when your project's own data cannot answer a data question. Not on passing mentions.

The case against, and what would make me wrong

  • A web tool invites lazy "just Google it" answers that skip the project's own numbers. The fire-only-when-asked rule and a tables-first rule are the guard; the test battery is the proof.
  • More sourced-looking text is more to invent around. The citation rule and the "nothing retrieved" rule are the guard; today showed the empty path is where invention starts.
  • If most county portals turn out to be search forms a plain fetch cannot drive, slice 1 shrinks to news and PDFs. Washoe is not like that; Maricopa's portal may be — I will check a second jurisdiction before generalising.
  • Keeping research outside the app (your 1b) is cheaper and already works. You chose 1a because Landscape is sold as the analyst, and this is the first thing a land buyer asks.
